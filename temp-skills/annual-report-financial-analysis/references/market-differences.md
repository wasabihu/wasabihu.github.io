# Market Differences

Use this file when the user asks about A-share, Hong Kong, or U.S. listed companies, or when the annual report format clearly maps to one of those markets.

## A-share

Typical documents are annual reports under mainland exchange and CSRC disclosure rules. They are usually more standardized in chapter order and often emphasize major accounting data, operating review, corporate governance, related-party transactions, and board or supervisory matters.

Prioritize these checks:

- non-recurring profit and loss versus recurring earnings quality
- government subsidies and whether profit depends on them
- top customer or supplier concentration
- receivables, inventory, and capitalized costs or capex quality
- related-party transactions, guarantees, funds occupation, or controller risk
- controlling-shareholder share pledge, freeze, or dilution risk

Interpretation notes:

- Do not rely on net profit alone; check cash flow and non-recurring items.
- Industrial policy, local subsidies, and expansion projects can materially affect quality of earnings.
- For manufacturing-heavy issuers, inventory, receivables, and fixed-asset turnover usually deserve more weight.

## Hong Kong

Typical documents are annual reports under HKEX listing rules, usually with IFRS or HKFRS-based financial statements, governance disclosures, and often stronger connected-transaction language than mainland reports. Many issuers also use offshore holding structures and adjusted metrics in management discussion.

Prioritize these checks:

- connected transactions and continuing connected transactions
- IFRS fair-value movements that inflate or depress earnings
- adjusted or non-IFRS metrics versus statutory profit
- offshore holding structure and cash repatriation practicality
- governance consistency, auditor issues, and ESG narrative credibility
- debt, covenant, refinancing, and property revaluation exposure when relevant

Interpretation notes:

- Separate statutory profit from fair-value-driven gains or losses.
- Watch whether management relies on adjusted metrics without a clear bridge back to reported numbers.
- Cross-border structures can make cash access, minority interests, and related-party exposure more important.

## U.S.

Typical documents are Form 10-K filings under SEC rules. They are more explicit on risk factors, MD&A, legal proceedings, internal controls, and non-GAAP reconciliation. The filing is often longer, more litigation-aware, and more detailed on accounting judgments.

Prioritize these checks:

- Item 1A risk factors and whether they changed materially year over year
- MD&A quality and whether management addresses margin, demand, and cash conversion clearly
- non-GAAP measures and reconciliation discipline
- stock-based compensation, dilution, and buyback economics
- goodwill, intangible assets, impairment risk, and acquisition integration
- internal control weaknesses, auditor emphasis, or legal and regulatory exposure

Interpretation notes:

- Give more weight to cash flow, SBC burden, and segment economics than headline EPS alone.
- A longer risk-factor section is not automatically worse; focus on what changed and what became more specific.
- For acquisitive companies, impairment and integration quality matter as much as revenue growth.

## Cross-market comparison rule

When comparing companies across these markets:

- normalize for currency, accounting framework, and one-off items before comparing margins or valuation
- do not treat adjusted profit, non-recurring profit, and non-GAAP earnings as equivalent without reconciliation
- state clearly if your comparison is limited by different disclosure depth or accounting treatment
